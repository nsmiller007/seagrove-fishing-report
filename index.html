General tips (any time of year):

  • Moving tides — fish the 2 hours on either side of high or low tide
  • Early mornings — the first 2–3 hours after sunrise are often most productive
  • Late afternoons into dusk — another prime low-light feeding period
  • Low light & overcast — redfish and trout feed more confidently with less glare

Check tide and solunar tables (see Tides4Fishing / FishingReminder links below) for exact major/minor periods.

🎣 Popular Fishing Spots on 30A

Inshore / Bayou

Hogtown Bayou

One of the best inshore spots near 30A. Grass flats, oyster bars, and points. Excellent for redfish and trout from kayak or shore at Cessna Landing.

Coastal Dune Lake

Western Lake (Grayton)

Unique brackish lake with bass, bream, and occasional reds/trout near the outfall. Great kayak access from Grayton Beach State Park.

Surf

Seagrove Beach

Classic Gulf surf fishing. Target pompano, whiting, and occasional redfish in the troughs and near sandbars. Multiple public accesses.

Surf + Lake

Grayton Beach

Combination of surf fishing and Western Lake outfall. Good mix of species and very scenic. State park access.

Surf

Inlet Beach

Less crowded surf option on the east end of 30A. Solid pompano and whiting action, especially near the state park area.

Inshore

Eastern Lake Outflow

Another productive coastal dune lake. Fish the connection to the Gulf for reds, trout, and flounder on moving tides.

Bay Flats

Choctawhatchee Bay (331 Area)

Big grass flats and structure north of 30A. Great for sight fishing reds and trout from kayak. Launch from Bay Baits area or public ramps.

Surf

Topsail Hill Preserve

More remote and less pressured beach access. Good for surf fishing with fewer crowds. Long walk to the water in some areas.

Target Species & Proven Tactics

🐟 Redfish (Red Drum)

Best in Choctawhatchee Bay grass flats, oyster bars, Hogtown Bayou, creek mouths, and dock lines. Sight fishing excellent on calm days.

Top Lures & Rigs:
  • Gold Weedless Spoon (¼ oz Johnson Silver Minnow) — #1 local choice. Slow retrieve over grass & oysters.
  • Gulp! Shrimp or Swimming Mullet (3–5") on ⅛–¼ oz jighead — pearl, chartreuse, root beer.
  • Topwater (Zara Spook, walking baits) at dawn/dusk or overcast.
  • Chatterbait / Skimmer Jig in stained water or around structure.

Pro tip: Troll a gold spoon behind a kayak while exploring flats — locals say it’s almost unfair.

→ More redfish lure advice (Florida Sportsman)

🐟 Speckled Seatrout

Grass flats, sandy potholes, deeper edges (4–7 ft), and creek mouths. Very consistent right now.

Best Tactics:
  • Topwater at first light on higher tides
  • Gulp shrimp or 3–4" paddle tails under popping cork
  • Live shrimp or pinfish on light jighead
  • CAL jigs or similar in pearl/chartreuse

Target deeper grass edges once boat traffic and sun get high.

🐟 Pompano (Surf)

Seagrove Beach and nearby 30A surf. Good reports in late spring/early summer when weeds aren’t thick.

Surf Rigs:
  • Double-drop pompano rig with pink/white/orange beads
  • Fresh or frozen shrimp, sand fleas (coquinas), Fishbites
  • Small jigs or pompano spoons in troughs between bars
